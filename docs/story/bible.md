# The Fifth Guild — Story Bible

**Status:** Reference document for the overarching narrative, world arcs, and combat encounters of Avalon. Governs all diegetic copy across the story spine, world episodes, boss encounters, contract postings, and the Case File.

## 1. The World and The Truce

Four Imperial Guilds divide the frontier into extractive monopolies:
- **Mineral Mining (Earth / Terra):** Sinks deep boreholes into lifeless rock, strip-mining iron, silicates, and heavy ores.
- **The Atmospheric Harvesters (Air / Zephyr):** Skims the toxic upper envelopes of gas giants and storm basins for noble gases, nitrogen, and volatile fuels.
- **The Thermal Smelters (Fire / Ignis):** Operates orbital crucibles, geothermal blast-wells, and induction smelters that crack crude ore into working alloy.
- **Moisture Extraction (Water / Thalassa):** Drills dry seabeds and frozen subsurfaces, filtering brine, heavy water, and industrial coolants.

To touch any world on the rim, a vessel needs an **Imperial Charter**. Without one, landing is piracy and salvage is contraband.

The player is a junior **survey hand** aboard the ***Avalon***: a forty-year-old heavy hauler operating under an uneasy, decades-old quad-guild truce. The *Avalon* is an industrial workhorse held together by cold rivets, copper pipe, asbestos-lagged steam lines, and stamped brass brackets. It smells of hot lubricating oil, dried sea-salt, and scorched vacuum insulation.

### The Voice: Quartermaster Vess
Vess is the beating heart of the *Avalon*: never a face, only a backlit silhouette in the comms bay window and a low, measured voice crackling over the tactical speaker. Vess is neither a cheerleader nor an adversary. Vess is the seasoned quartermaster who knows every bolt on the ship, has logged twenty seasons of dead survey runs, and evaluates a rookie not by test scores, but by whether their hands stay steady when the dials peg red.

## 2. The Premise: The Ghost Charter

A fifth presence has surfaced along the outer sectors. It flies no guild livery, carries no Imperial charter, and transmits on emergency carrier frequencies dead for thirty years. Crews across the mess decks call it **The Fifth**.

Reports from forward extraction sites don't make sense:
- Smelter installations abandoned overnight with their fireboxes still burning.
- Slag heaps and tailings dumps that twitch, crystallize, and **walk under their own power**.
- Refinery storehouses reporting inventories that violate the laws of mass: iron converted into lead, pure rock salt sweating gold, and output manifolds producing heavier tonnage than the raw ore fed into the hoppers.

Whispers run through every canteen on the rim: *the Fifth has discovered alchemy.*

## 3. The Chemistry Truth: The Detective Kit

Avalon does not use story to distract from chemistry; **the chemistry is the detective kit that solves the mystery.**

1. **The Law of Elements (Tallow / Unit 1):** Early on, the student discovers that an element cannot be split into simpler substances by mechanical shear, heat, or standard reagents (`q1-grain`), and that the atomic number of the nucleus dictates the identity of the atom (`q2-core`). The mess deck rumor of turning salt into precious metals is fundamentally **impossible** by chemical means.
2. **Definite Proportions & Conservation (Kettle / Unit 7):** Chemical reactions strictly conserve mass and combine in discrete integer ratios (`q1-recipe`). A ledger showing mass appearing from nowhere cannot be a chemical process.
3. **The Reveal (Cinder Halo / Unit 14):** The Fifth is not practicing chemistry at all. They have reactivated an ancient sub-stellar **transmutation reactor** on Cinder Halo. They are breaking and rearranging the **atomic nucleus** itself — physics that is real, catastrophic, and radioactive. They are consuming whole worlds as fuel to forge raw matter from scrap.

A thoughtful player can deduce the nature of the Fifth long before reaching Cinder Halo. Every clue collected in the **Case File** points to the same inescapable conclusion: ordinary electron-cloud chemistry cannot explain the anomalies; only subatomic restructuring can.

## 4. The Catalyst: The Abandoned Manifest

Who is the Fifth?

They are **the forgotten crews the Guilds wrote off.**

When a mineral vein thinned out on Erebus, or an atmospheric pocket fouled on Lanternfall, the Guild accountants calculated that the fuel cost of an evacuation ship exceeded the value of the charter. The Guilds declared those stations "depleted," stamped the manifests *abandoned due to environmental attrition*, and left hundreds of miners, assayers, and engineers to suffocate or freeze in the dark.

Those crews did not die. They scavenged dropped tools, jury-rigged extraction manifolds, pooled their survival gear, and slowly migrated toward the graveyard world of Cinder Halo.

### The Name
In Unit 11 (**Quicklight**), the player earns the reward card defining a **catalyst**: *a substance that increases the rate of a reaction without undergoing any permanent chemical change itself.*

At that exact moment, the Fifth's true title is uncovered: **The Catalyst**.

Like a chemical catalyst, they lower the activation barrier for revolt across the frontier, igniting rebellions on forgotten outposts while leaving no signature on the official ledgers.

## 5. Narrative Structure: Four Acts

```
Act I: THE CHARTER (Units 1–3: Tallow, Lanternfall, Ligar)
  Routine surveys reveal mobile spoil and unliveried stencils.
  Spine Beat 1: Guild Inquiry arrives; officers order silence.

Act II: PRESSURE (Units 4–7: Bellows, Ember Reach, Mirrowen, Kettle)
  The Fifth recruits active workers; Kettle's impossible ledger is seized.
  Spine Beat 2: The Avalon's survey logs are leaking to the Fifth.

Act III: BALANCE (Units 8–11: Teeter, Verdigris, Brinewire, Quicklight)
  Equilibrium fails; acid leaks spread; the name "The Catalyst" is uncovered.
  Spine Beat 3: Corporate abandonment exposed; Vess confronts the crew.

Act IV: THE CORE (Units 12–14: Lodestone, Thornmere, Cinder Halo)
  Following thermal signatures to the transmutation works at Cinder Halo.
  Spine Beat 4: The Finale — The Transmutation Core, the choice, the legacy.
```

### Episode Kit per World
Every world acts as a self-contained investigative episode:
1. **Arrival Transmission:** One laconic message from Vess over the speaker (max 2 sentences). Frames the breakdown without hinting at puzzle mechanics.
2. **The Spoil:** A family of animated minerals born directly from that world's chemistry principles.
3. **Bench Encounters:** Every stage framed as an analytical confrontation where measurement dictates victory.
4. **Boss / Elite:** The world's conceptual synthesis; multi-phase checkpointed combat against a colossal rogue machine or mineral mass.
5. **The Case File Clue:** A piece of physical evidence yielded upon boss defeat.
6. **Closing Transmission:** Vess logs the sector status and files the findings.

## 6. The Spoil: Living Chemistry

Spoil is not magic. It is refined tailings and waste residues animated by electromagnetic leakage and radiological seepage from the Fifth's testing grounds.

### Rules of Spoil Combat
1. **Physical Obedience:** Spoil behaves strictly according to the chemical laws measured at the bench. A mixture separates when agitated; a covalent network lattice resists thermal shear; a photoelectric shell releases charges only above threshold frequency.
2. **Dispersed, Never Slaughtered:** Foes crumble into inert salt, drift into cold clouds, or freeze into dormant stone. There is no gore, no cruelty, and no blood.
3. **Honest Lighting (SCOURED PLATE):** Monochromatic cast-iron bodies lit only by amber filament glows, spark discharges, and authentic elemental emission spectra. No glowing red evil eyes. No neon fantasy aura.

### Spoil Roster
- **Tallow (Unit 1):** *Salt Husks* — crystalline aggregates of pure elements, molecular compounds, and heterogeneous grit. Weak to mechanical cutting, physical sorting, and mass verification.
- **Lanternfall (Unit 2):** *Lamp Moths & Frost Wraiths* — photoelectric shells and quantized gas clouds. Weak to precise electromagnetic frequencies and matching emission bands.
- **Ligar (Unit 3):** *Basalt Wardens & Slag Crawlers* — bonded mineral armor. Weak to thermal shock, shear stress, and electrical conductivity testing based on lattice geometry.
- **Bellows (Unit 4):** *Gas Bladders* — skinned bags of gas that sag, hold, swell and strain with the push inside them, and a rogue **Compressor** at the heart of the deck. Settled by reading the gauge, never by bursting them: a bladder strains at its seams and is dispersed, never popped.
- **Kettle (Unit 7):** *Recipe Golems* — rigidly proportioned stoichiometric monoliths. Disrupted only by starving their limiting reagents.

## 7. The Clues and The Case File

The Case File is the investigative terminal in the *Avalon*'s comms bay (and `#/casefile` in the UI). Clues are **collected, never graded**. The game never insults the player with a multiple-choice quiz about what a clue means. The player examines the evidence, looks at the periodic table, and realizes the truth on their own.

1. **Erebus (Contract 1):** *The Unliveried Stencil* — A maintenance plate bearing an unchartered delta mark instead of the four Imperial Guild insignias.
2. **Tallow (Boss 1 - The Hopper):** *The Transmuted Slag* — A refined ingot from a sodium chloride salt pan containing traces of elemental bismuth that were never present in the geological basin.
3. **Lanternfall (Boss 2 - The Lantern-Eater):** *The Ground Optical Lens* — A focusing prism ground for deep ultraviolet wavelengths that no standard guild lamp can emit.
4. **Ligar (Elite 1 - Basalt Warden):** *The Arc-Sheared Basalt* — A quarry slab cut cleanly through molecular and ionic matrixes by thermal energy exceeding any known refinery torch.
5. **Kettle (Elite 2 - Recipe Golem):** *The Over-Mass Ledger* — A logistics logbook documenting reaction products whose mass reliably exceeds the total mass of the reactants.
6. **Bellows (Boss - The Compressor):** *The Warm Cylinder* — A sealed gas cylinder logged at one pressure for twelve days while the deck froze fifty degrees around it. Gay-Lussac's law forbids a sealed gas to do that unless something inside it is giving off heat, and no chemical reaction does for twelve days. It points at Cinder Halo.

## 8. Voice and Discipline

- **Diegetic Imperative:** Every transmission comes from a character, a ship intercom, or a field telemetry terminal. Terms like "user," "button," "level," "menu," or "game" are banned from narrative strings.
- **The Two-Sentence Limit:** No transmission may exceed two sentences. Long text walls destroy urgency.
- **Strict Vocabulary Schedule:** No concept may be named in dialogue or debrief copy before the stage that introduces it. "Catalyst" belongs strictly to Unit 11; "Nucleus" belongs strictly to Unit 1 Quest 2.
- **No Route Hints:** Dialogue sets the atmosphere and urgency; it never tells the student which dial to turn or which sample to select.
- **Critical Text Only & Unadorned Identity:** Speakers use simple, unadorned names (`Vess`, not `VESS // QUARTERMASTER`). Banners and transmissions strip pseudo-technical `//` delimiters, fake status tags (`status: clear`, `integrity: high`), and decorative jargon. Every word must be necessary, helpful, and meaningful to the student.
