The question is about dating the camp; stages 1 to 6 ask about iodine-131 and stage 7 about an old bone, never about the camp's samples. Stage 7 should be "how long has this sample been here" about something from the camp, not a bone.
**q5, stage 1, tour.** The tour (briefing then "DECAYING SAMPLE") opened on my first visit with 2 steps: "Each radioactive nucleus has the same chance of decaying in any day, whatever has happened before. The grid shows 256 nuclei of iodine-131 on one clock." and "A grid of radioactive nuclei: a bright one has not decayed, a dark one has. Tap "Wait" to move the clock on and "Back" to move it back; the plot beside the grid marks what is left at every step so far." Good, specific. No pointer showing the press of Wait.
**q5, stage 1, bench.** Best bench so far for readability: 256 orange dots, black for decayed, a plot that fills in as the clock moves, "128 of 256 left" under the grid, "Clock: 8 days." The line "A large sample; a few nuclei alone do not keep to this." is small italic text under the clock and not understood by a beginner; say "With only a few nuclei the numbers would jump around".
**q5, stage 1, answer.** The prompt asks "How many days pass before half of the iodine-131 nuclei have decayed?" but there is no answer field: the answer is the day the clock shows when Submit is pressed. Nothing says that. Add "Stop the clock on the day, then Submit" to the prompt or add a typed box.
**q5, stage 1, miss.** At day 3: "On day 3 the grid holds 198 of the 256." States the count only. Add "That is more than half (128)".
**q5, stage 1, reward.** "Half-Life. Eight days: 128 of the 256 are left. The time for half of the nuclei of a radioactive sample to decay is its half-life." Good.
**q5, stage 1, nuclei.** Grid is 256 nuclei with a tiny random scatter ("198 at day 3" against 256 x 0.77 = 197), fine. The grid does not show whether a nucleus is "about to" decay; fine.
**q5, stage 2, tour/bench.** No tour. Predict stage with cards "None of it / A quarter of it / Still half of it". The prediction about "after 16 days" comes straight after stage 1 where the clock went to day 8; the student could run the clock to 16 at once, so the prediction is a single click's worth of waiting. Fine as a predict stage, but move the grid onto the predict stage (clock locked at day 8) so the student sees the 128 left before guessing.
**q5, stage 2, hint.** "Each nucleus still there has the same chance of decaying as on the first day." The one hint is the whole idea. Same problem as the other predict stages: the Hint button should not exist on a predict stage.
**q5, stage 2, reward.** "Prediction recorded. Your prediction is recorded. Run the clock." Duplicate sentence again.
**q5, stage 3, tour.** None.
**q5, stage 3, bench.** Same grid as stage 1 with three answer cards ("None / A quarter / A half") at the right. Reaching day 16 takes 16 presses of "Wait 1 day"; add a "Wait 8 days" key (or a slider) as the stage only needs days 8 and 16.
**q5, stage 3, answer wording.** The options "A quarter" and "A half" do not say of what; "A quarter of 256" or "64 of 256" would be clearer. The student must work out 64 / 256 = a quarter in the head.
**q5, stage 3, miss.** "At day 16 the grid still holds 64 nuclei." Good, but it does not say that 64 is a quarter of 256.
**q5, stage 3, reward.** "A quarter (you predicted none): in the second 8 days half of the 128 still there decayed, leaving 64. Each half-life halves what is left, so it never reaches zero in a whole half-life." The last sentence is confusing ("never reaches zero in a whole half-life"); say "so the sample is never all gone after any whole number of half-lives".
**q5, stage 3, hint 3.** Not opened.
**q5, stage 4, tour.** None; two grids side by side with no explanation of the second one.
**q5, stage 4, bench.** The nuclei are yellow here and orange in stages 1 to 3; use one colour. The plot draws both samples as counts, so the 64 sample is a flat strip at the bottom and the 256 sample a long slope; they do not look like the same half-life, which is the point. Plot "fraction left" for both on one scale, or draw each grid's own plot.
**q5, stage 4, repeats.** Stage 4 asks whether the half-life changes with the amount; the same stage 1 grid at a different size. Good idea; but the student only learns "neither" by running the clock to day 8 (8 clicks), which a student can skip because the miss message hands over the numbers ("On day 8 the large grid holds 128 of 256 and the small grid 32 of 64"). Make the miss message ask the student to run the clock to 8 first.
**q5, stage 4, miss.** Chose "The 256" without running the clock and got the day-8 counts for both grids: that is the whole answer, handed over for free.
**q5, stage 4, reward.** "Neither: both halved in 8 days. The half-life does not depend on how much there is, the mark of a first-order process." "first-order process" is introduced in a sentence with no explanation; say "So the half-life is a property of the isotope, not of the amount".
**q5, stage 5, tour.** None. The typed box "Nuclei left" has no unit and is fine.
**q5, stage 5, bench.** Same grid as stage 1, clock runs only to day 24 (the plot's right edge) but the prompt asks about 40 days, which cannot be reached on the clock. That is the point (extend the pattern), but nothing on screen says "this clock stops at 24 days". Say so in the prompt.
**q5, stage 5, miss.** Typed 0: "At day 24 the grid still holds 32, and each 8 days leaves half of what was there." The student may never have run the clock to day 24; the message quotes a count they did not see. Say "Run the clock to day 24: 32 are left."
**q5, stage 5, solve.** 256 halved five times is quick arithmetic that does not need the grid or the bench; this is a maths exercise (halve five times). Fine as a practice step but it adds no new chemistry: cut it, or ask for a number of half-lives that is not a whole number (for example 20 days) to need the plot.
**q5, stage 5, reward.** "Five half-lives: 8 of 256 left. Eight: forty days is five half-lives, and 256 halved five times is 128, 64, 32, 16, then 8." Good.
**q5, stage 6, briefing.** Opens by itself: "For first-order decay the rate constant k is the fraction of the nuclei that decay in each day, for short times, and t½ = 0.693 / k. The plot here is ln of the nuclei left, which falls in a straight line." That is the densest text in the world: "first-order", "rate constant", "for short times", "ln" and a formula in two sentences, for a 12-year-old. Break into: what k means (a bigger k, faster decay), then the formula, and leave out "ln" and "first-order" here or explain them. The worked case (cobalt-60: 0.693 ÷ 5.27 = 0.132) is clear.
**q5, stage 6, bench.** The briefing box covers the "First order: t½ = 0.693 / k" plate while it is open. The plot's axis is "ln(nuclei left)" and is empty at day 0; the student can answer without touching the clock or the plot (half-life 8 days was found in stage 1, the formula is on the plate), so the ln plot is decoration. Either drop the plot or make the answer come from its slope.
**q5, stage 6, giveaway.** The plate "t½ = 0.693 / k" plus stage 1's 8 days gives k = 0.693 / 8 in one step. The miss message for 5.55 reads "That is 0.693 × 8; the plate divides 0.693 by k." which spells out the operation. Replace with "k is small for a long half-life; check which way your answer goes".
**q5, stage 6, reward.** "0.0866 per day: 0.693 ÷ 8. A bigger rate constant means a shorter half-life, and ln of what is left falls by k every day." The ln sentence is unexplained; remove.
**q5, stage 6, answer tolerance.** 0.0866 accepted; a student who types 0.087 (rounding) also passes (range 0.085 to 0.0875): fine.
**q5, stage 7, briefing.** Opens by itself: "A living thing keeps the same share of carbon-14 as the air, about 15.3 counts per minute for each gram of its carbon. Once it dies none is added, and its carbon-14 halves every 5,730 years." with a worked case (a bone counts 7.7, half of 15.3, so 5,730 years). Clear, and it puts the whole method in the briefing, so the stage is a copy of the worked case with one more halving. A student who skips the briefing has no way to know 15.3 or 5,730 (they are not on the bench).
**q5, stage 7, bench.** "Carbon-14 after death" grid and a "The old bone: 1.9 per minute per gram" plate (the plate is half hidden under the briefing box and, afterwards, shows fine). The clock moves in 1,000-year steps ("Wait 1,000 years"), but the answer, 17,190, falls between steps (17,000 and 18,000) and the clock only goes to 12,000 years, so the clock cannot reach the answer: the bench is not used to solve it. Extend the clock to 24,000 and let the student stop it where the count reads 1.9.
**q5, stage 7, not the question.** The question card asked about the camp's samples; a bone with no link to the camp is unrelated. Make the sample something from the crater camp (a burnt log in the camp's fire pit).
**q5, stage 7, miss.** Typed 5730: "After 5,730 years the bone would count 7.7, half the living count." Good, specific. Hint 3 unopened.
**q5, stage 7, reward.** "Radiocarbon Dating. About 17,190 years: 15.3 halves to 7.7, 3.8, then 1.9, three half-lives." Good. Note: the accepted range 16,900 to 17,500 is tight enough that 17,000 is refused, which is a fair rounding; allow 17,000.
**q5, stage 8, tour.** None; the bench has a new clock in hours and a "Half-lives" plate, no explanation.
**q5, stage 8, bench.** Clear: a plate of three half-lives (fluorine-18: 1.8 hours, technetium-99m: 6.0 hours, iodine-131: 8.0 days), a clock in hours, the count rate printed under the grid ("6 hours · count rate 801 counts per minute") and a plot. The unit mix (hours against days) is a good trap. The plate and the answer list (same three names in the same order) hand the student three labelled choices; reorder one list.
**q5, stage 8, miss.** Chose fluorine-18 before touching the clock: "In 2 hours the vial's count rate fell from 1,600 to about 1,270, not to half." The student has not run the clock; the message quotes numbers from a run they did not do. Say "Run the clock first".
**q5, stage 8, solve.** Running the clock to 6 hours gives 801 from 1,600 (half), clean. The grid also shows the nuclei going dark; the plot is not needed.
**q5, stage 8, reward.** "Its Own Signature. Technetium-99m: the count rate halved every 6 hours. A half-life belongs to the isotope, so it tells you which isotope you have." Good.
**q5, debrief.** Four slides. 1 "One half-life": a 16 x 16 grid at day 8, "128 of 256 left", caption "In 8 days half of the 256 iodine nuclei decayed, leaving 128." 2 "Half again": the grid at day 16 with 64 left. Both are still pictures; the animation of nuclei going dark (the real mechanism) is not played. 3 "The carbon clock": a text list "0 years: 15.3 ... 17,190 years: 1.9 per minute per gram" with no picture at all; draw the bone's count falling or a bar halving three times. 4 case card: "Counting half-lives in their samples shows the Fifth Guild has worked here for decades." The quest only counted iodine-131 days and one bone's thousands of years; nothing in the quest dated a sample from the camp, so "for decades" is an unsupported conclusion and the story question ("how long have they worked here") is answered with no number. Give the camp sample a count rate in stage 7 and let the student date it.
**q5, in general.** (1) The grid bench (stages 1, 3, 4, 5) is the clearest bench in the world; keep. (2) Stage 1 has no answer field (answer = where the clock stops). (3) Stages 6 and 7 stack heavy text in a briefing and then ask the student to repeat the worked case with one number changed; neither uses the bench. (4) The clock in days costs 8 to 16 clicks; add "Wait 8 days". (5) Four of the miss messages quote counts the student never produced (stages 5, 7 as a "fell from" line, 8). (6) The story question is not answered by any stage. (7) Predict stage 2 has the same one-hint design as the other predict stages.

## Bench 6: The Missing Weight (q6-shortfall)

**q6, opening.** Question card: "The Fifth's reactor puts out huge amounts of energy, yet no fuel line runs into it. Where does the energy come from?" Good link to the world's story. But stage 1 then asks about the weight of a helium-4 nucleus, which is not obviously the reactor's energy; stage 1 should say "the energy may be hidden in the weight".
**q6, stage 1, tour/bench.** No tour. The quest opens on a predict stage with nothing to look at: three cards (More / The same / Less than its parts) and a slot. The student is asked to predict a nuclear mass without having seen a balance or been told what "its parts" weigh. Put the balance on this stage with the pans empty so the student sees what will be weighed.
**q6, stage 1, giveaway.** Hint 1, the only hint: "A nucleus holds together, so pulling its protons and neutrons apart takes energy." Gives half the reasoning before the prediction. Remove the Hint button on predict stages.
**q6, stage 1, reward.** "Prediction recorded. Your prediction is recorded. Weigh them." Same duplicate.
**q6, stage 2, tour.** Opens by itself: "BALANCE: Two pans on a beam that tips toward the heavier; each pan prints its mass in u (one u is about the mass of one proton). Where it has keys, tap "Add Proton" or "Add Neutron" to load the left pan." Short and good. "u" is not explained beyond "about the mass of one proton"; fine.
**q6, stage 2, bench.** A flat dark panel with a thin beam, two pans at the ends and numbers in small amber text ("4.031882 u", "4.001506 u"). The beam tilts a little; the difference between the pans (0.03 u, under 1 %) would not tip a real balance this much and the tilt is exaggerated without saying so. The protons and neutrons dropped on the left pan are 4 dark red dots a few pixels wide. Make the pans' contents big and add a label "tilt not to scale".
**q6, stage 2, instrument.** Add Proton and Add Neutron work and the pan sum updates; there is no key to remove one (use Undo). The student must know a helium-4 nucleus is two protons and two neutrons: the prompt says "the protons and neutrons it is made of" and stage 1's prompt says it, fine.
**q6, stage 2, miss.** Answering "The same" with an empty pan: "The left pan is empty: nothing has been weighed against the nucleus." Good.
**q6, stage 2, answer order.** Options are "The same / More / Less": correct answer last. Fine.
**q6, stage 2, reward.** "Mass Defect. Less (you predicted the same): 0.030376 u less than two protons and two neutrons apart. The mass a nucleus lacks against its separate parts is its mass defect." Good. The picture is a very small atom model in a large empty card; make it show the four pieces and a gap labelled "missing 0.0304 u".
**q6, stage 3, briefing.** Opens by itself: "Mass and energy are one thing in two forms, E = mc²: 1 u of mass is 931.5 MeV of energy, where an MeV is a unit of energy sized for one nucleus. Deuterium, one proton and one neutron, lacks 0.002388 u." with a worked case. The first mention of E = mc² and the first definition of MeV, in one box, to a student who has never met energy units at nuclear scale; "MeV" needs one picture or sentence ("a million times smaller than a joule" is no help; say "the energy unit for one nucleus"). The worked case is clear and mirrors the stage.
**q6, stage 3, bench.** The balance now has both pans filled ("2 protons + 2 neutrons 4.031882 u" and "Helium-4 nucleus 4.001506 u") and a "Mass as energy: 1 u = 931.5 MeV" plate, half hidden under the briefing while it is open. The task is subtract, multiply: a calculator exercise with the chemistry in the briefing. No visible link from the bench to the answer apart from two printed numbers.
**q6, stage 3, miss.** Typed 2.22: "2.22 MeV is deuterium's, from the worked case." Good. Hint 3 not opened.
**q6, stage 3, reward.** "Binding Energy. 28.3 MeV: the mass defect as energy. The energy needed to pull a nucleus apart into its protons and neutrons is its binding energy." Good. It does not connect back to the question card (where the reactor's energy comes from); that happens in stage 5.
**q6, stage 3, accepted range.** 28.1 to 28.5; a student who types 28 is refused with "28 MeV is the energy of 0.030060 u of mass; the balance shows a different difference." Accept 28.
**q6, stage 4, tour.** Two steps. 1 "Binding energy per nucleon is a nucleus's binding energy shared out over its protons and neutrons: the higher it is, the more tightly each one is held." (a heavy new term in a sentence that defines itself with "binding energy", which stage 3 defined). 2 "BINDING-ENERGY CURVE: Each point is one nucleus: its mass number across, and how tightly each of its protons and neutrons is held up the side. Tap a point to read it, or to choose it as your answer." Good. No pointer.
**q6, stage 4, bench.** The curve fills the panel and is readable at the top end, but the axes are tiny ("MeV per nucleon", "50 100 150 200", "Mass number →"). The points (about 25) are 3 px dots and are hard to hit; the peak points near mass 56 to 62 sit within a few pixels of each other. Enlarge the dots and label hovered points.
**q6, stage 4, instrument.** Tapping a point prints "Uranium-235: mass number 235, 7.6 MeV per nucleon" at the bottom left; this works and is the useful part.
**q6, stage 4, solve.** The curve's peak is visible without reading a number; the stage is "tap the highest dot". The miss gives the position away: "points round mass number 60 stand higher". Say "a higher point exists" and leave the region to the student.
**q6, stage 4, reward.** "Iron-56 and nickel-62, at 8.8 MeV for each nucleon. Nuclei near iron are the most tightly bound; lighter and heavier ones hold theirs less tightly." Good. The curve in the card is not shown again; show the two sides ("join" and "split") arrows on it, which the next stages need.
**q6, stage 5, tour.** Two steps: briefing ("When a slow neutron enters a uranium-235 nucleus, the nucleus can split in two. The balance weighs everything before and after."), then "CHAIN CHAMBER: A block of uranium-235. Press "Fire Neutron" to send one neutron in... Where it has rod keys, they set how many of the three neutrons each split frees the control rods soak up." The second step opened by itself later, after I pressed Fire Neutron and while I was reading the miss, so it covered the chamber at the moment the split played. It also describes rod keys that are not on this bench; cut that sentence here.

But the balance already prints "Before 236.052595 u / After 235.866571 u" before any neutron is fired, so "after < before" can be read without using the chamber; show the "After" pan empty ("?") until the split has run. The "Fire Neutron" key is tiny and dark (red on near-black) like the Count key.
**q6, stage 5, instrument.** The split plays as two blobs ("barium-141", "krypton-92") and a small dot flying out, with the line "Split: barium-141, krypton-92 and 3 neutrons". The three neutrons are not visibly three; make them fly out. The nucleus and fragments are flat dark-red discs.
**q6, stage 5, miss.** "The balance reads 236.052595 u before and 235.866571 u after." States the numbers and not which is smaller; for a student who reads decimals badly this is no help. Say "After is smaller by 0.186 u: where did it go?".
**q6, stage 5, reward.** "Fission... Out: the pieces weigh 0.186 u less than the uranium and its neutron. A heavy nucleus splitting into two lighter ones is fission." Good. It does not say the missing mass became energy here (the stage 3 idea); add "0.186 u is about 173 MeV".
**q6, stage 6, tour/briefing.** Briefing opens by itself: "A fission like the one you watched frees three neutrons, and each can split another nucleus. Control rods soak up neutrons." Good, short. No other tour.
**q6, stage 6, cannot test the answer.** The rod keys are "Soak up none", "Soak up one" and "Soak up all three"; the answer is two, which has no key. A student who sets the rods to one (doubles: 1, 2, 4, 8) and to all three (stops after one fission) must guess "two" by thinking in between. Add a "Soak up two" key so the right answer can be seen holding steady (1, 1, 1, 1).
**q6, stage 6, bench.** Four columns drawn as dots with captions "1 fission / 3 fissions / 9 fissions / 27 fissions" (none soaked) and, when rods are in, grey vertical bars between generations; the dots are small dark-red and hard to count, so the captions do the work. Make each fission a bright dot and make the neutrons visible as lines between generations. The "Fire Neutron" key is dim again.
**q6, stage 6, miss.** Chose 1: "With one soaked up, each generation doubled: 1, 2, 4, 8." Good, specific; the student had run exactly that.
**q6, stage 6, answer list.** The four options are "0 / 1 / 2 / 3" in a column under "Soaked up", which is understandable; but the label says "Soaked up" without "of three".
**q6, stage 6, reward.** "Chain Reaction. Two: then each fission causes exactly one more. A run of fissions whose neutrons cause more is a chain reaction, and one that holds steady is critical." The two new words (chain reaction, critical) are in one sentence; "critical" means something else in daily speech; add "critical means steady".
**q6, stage 7, tour.** None. No explanation of "fusion" before the stage; the idea comes in the reward.
**q6, stage 7, bench.** A balance (both pans already read "4.027106 u" and "4.001506 u", no loading needed) above a small "The curve" panel with only two labelled points near the left edge ("Helium-4", "Hydrogen-2") on a squashed plot where the labels overlap the axis. The curve panel is too small to see anything; the student gets the answer from the numbers on the balance alone.
**q6, stage 7, repeats stage 5.** Same idea as stage 5 (the side that weighs less gives energy out) with different nuclei. The only new thing is the word "fusion". Merge 5 and 7, or let the student choose from three reactions on the curve (fission of uranium, fusion of hydrogen, splitting iron) and say which ones give energy.
**q6, stage 7, miss.** "The balance reads 4.027106 u for two hydrogen-2 nuclei and 4.001506 u for helium-4." Numbers only; the student has to compare six-digit decimals. Print the difference ("0.0256 u less") on the balance.
**q6, stage 7, reward.** "Fusion. Joining them: helium-4 weighs 0.0256 u less and sits far higher on the curve. Light nuclei joining into a heavier one is fusion, which powers the stars." Good. "Which powers the stars" is a one-sentence aside that links to nothing in the quest.
**q6, stage 8, tour.** None. The stage is labelled "SEALED" in the plan but nothing looks sealed.
**q6, stage 8, repeats stage 4.** The same curve with three points marked X, Y and Z and the question "Which marked nucleus could give energy out neither by splitting nor by joining with another?" is "tap the highest point" again; stage 4 already asked for the highest point. Replace with a question that uses the fission and fusion ideas of stages 5 and 7 on unmarked points, or cut.
**q6, stage 8, prompt.** "Which marked nucleus could give energy out neither by splitting nor by joining with another?" is a double negative a 12-year-old will have to read three times. Say "Which nucleus cannot give out energy by splitting or by joining?".
**q6, stage 8, bench.** The curve fills the panel; the three marks are tiny white dots with a single letter (x, y, z) next to them, 6 px high. The point the student taps (Z, mass 238) is not exactly where the dot is drawn and the first tap read "Z: mass number 238, 7.6 MeV per nucleon" correctly. Y is where the curve peaks.

## Boss: The Transmutation Core (boss-core)

Then a title card "THE CORE PHASE 1 OF 5 / TRIALS / RIG THE CORE". "Rig the core" reads as sabotage; say "Shut down the core". The question card promises "turn one element into another" but no phase is about transmutation except phase 3's beta decay.
**boss, HUD.** A panel top right: "THE CORE  PHASE 1 OF 5", a red bar, "TRIALS" with five red dots and "RIG" with three dashes; a bar under the prompt "THE TRANSMUTATION CORE ■■■" with "A control rod drops home into the Core and its vessel cools a step." Nothing says what the dots (trials = lives?) or "RIG" mean, and the two bars repeat the same thing. Remove "RIG" or label it.
**boss, phase 1, tour/briefing.** Briefing opens: "The Core keeps three cans by its vents, one baking, one at deck temperature, one frozen. Only one holds a radioactive sample." Good. **boss, phase 1, repeat.** This is q1 stage 8 again (count three cards at three temperatures, find the radioactive one). Replace with a question that needs two ideas at once.
**boss, phase 1, miss.** "Trial missed: the reading does not match what the reactor shows." then "Can A counted 25, about the background." The first line is meaningless; delete it ("reactor shows" shows nothing).

The student reads "24 counts in one minute" beside "No sample" and may take it as the cask. Show "No sample in the slot" in red at Count time.
**boss, phase 2, miss.** Submitting an answer before counting: "The cask has not been counted open and behind paper." Good. After counting: "Paper took the cask only from 3,224 to 2,986." Good, specific. The trial dots drop from five to four on each miss.

## Cinder Halo: in general

**world, order and size.** 53 stages. Stages that are the same act again: q2 1/2/3, q2 7/8, q3 7/8, q4 4/5/6/8, q6 5/7, q6 4/8, boss 1 to 5 against benches 1 to 6. Cutting the repeats would remove about 15 stages and nothing would be lost.
**world, tour.** The generic "Counter" step (with shield slot, plates and keys not on the bench) and the generic "Sample cards" and "Your answer" steps appear on every counter bench; tours are absent on stages that introduce new controls (q3 stages 7 and 8, q5 stages 6 to 8, q6 stages 6 to 8). No tour step shows a pointer doing anything.
**world, predict stages.** Seven predict stages (q1 4, q2 4, q3 2, q4 2, q5 2, q6 1) have one hint, which gives the idea away, and a reward "Prediction recorded. Your prediction is recorded. ..." that says the same thing twice. Only q2 stage 4 says "Predictions are never graded".
**world, tiny and dim.** Count/Run/Fire Neutron keys, chart axes, card text and legend arrows are 5 to 8 px at 1440 wide; chart squares in q4 are dim grey on dark grey.
**world, miss messages.** Many quote numbers the student never produced (q1 s8, q5 s5, s8, s4; boss), or hand over the whole solution (q3 s8, q5 s6, q6 s5).
**world, story.** Each quest opens on a story question that the stages answer late or not at all (q5's camp samples, q6's fuel line); the debrief case cards sometimes say something the bench did not show (q2 "drums leak gamma rays", q5 "worked here for decades", boss "not alchemy").
