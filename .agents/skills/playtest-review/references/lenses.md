# What the owner notices

Drawn from the owner's audits (`docs/feedback.md` through `docs/feedback-11-21.md`). Each heading is a
kind of problem; each bullet is a real note, shortened. Use these to calibrate what counts as a note. They
are examples, not a checklist to tick: new kinds of problems are welcome.

## 1. Words a beginner cannot follow

- Vague or odd questions: "What does 'resolve' mean?"; "'Where round the nucleus' is so vague."
- Made-up or euphemistic names for real things: "room" for volume, "push" and "gauge" for pressure,
  "needle" for charge, "cutter", "shaker", "reader" (it is a scale), "Relay mast" (say "Radio"),
  "Tray the samples" (say "Sort the samples").
- Jargon in debriefs and warnings: "a charge must give more than the depth", "put in so far: 404",
  a warning that is "all gibberish I can't decipher".
- Terms used before they are introduced: "node", Kelvin, the ideal gas law hinted but never stated,
  "testing with lead", calibration, line of best fit, a "grey powder" never introduced.
- Labels that say nothing: "all solid" vs "hard crust" vs "tiny lump"; "Less time taken by ___" (use
  "Red is faster", "Violet is faster", "Neither is faster"); "Filed" story sections nobody understands.
- The question must follow from the premise: going from cutting basalt to "how much energy does it take
  to pull strong bonds apart?" is a non sequitur. Say "oxygen (8 electrons)" where the count matters.
- Rephrase to the plain version: "Would a triple bond between two carbons be shorter or longer than a
  single bond?"; "KE_electron = E_photon − W"; "If there are 72 grams of water and each mole weighs 18
  grams, then there are 4 moles of water."

## 2. Cannot operate it

- A piece cannot be picked back up (picking up the lens picks up the sample under it).
- No way to correct a mistake (an O=O bond cannot become O–O).
- No way to pass: no flip button; samples covered so the instrument cannot read them.
- A meter that never moves from 0; clicking does nothing; a dial that only moves in small jumps instead
  of dragging smoothly; a dial with only 200 and 400 when the answer is near 300.
- A control blocked by another piece (cannot turn the dial with the conductivity meter in the way).
- Calculator "Use value" fills "4.8e14" into a "___e14" box instead of "4.8".
- Unclear how to submit; leaving the bench leaves the stage bars on screen and the nav buttons dead.
- Instruments whose use is a mystery: "I have no clue how to operate it", "I have no clue what the needle
  does", "I can't figure out how to use the protractor intuitively" (clicking three atoms is better).

## 3. Giveaways

- The tour performs the answer ("power 4 is the answer").
- A word on the bench gives it away ("unchanged"); cards say "protons" when that is what is being found;
  the scale shows the element name.
- Matching colours of the plates under the atoms let you sort without thinking.
- Lines turn golden when right ("lines on the strip that fall on the line ___").

## 4. Busywork, duplicates, useless tools

- Dragging + to one side of a tray teaches nothing.
- A stage, bench or explainer that repeats an earlier one: "I'm pretty sure we already did it",
  "This bench feels the exact same as the previous bench".
- A tool that does not help solve the stage (the electron remover); a straightedge that shows no slope or
  intercept, or a line through the points without saying what a line of best fit is.
- Several detectors would be simpler than moving one detector around.
- Separate windows where a button beside the existing one would do ("Read element" next to "Tip Sample").

## 5. Layout and looks

- Overlaps from the player's point of view; fix by moving objects, not the camera.
- Labels cut off ("Sample A" partly hidden), text under a handle, labels hidden under trays.
- Cards that do not fit their slots, trays or "pulled"/"not pulled" boxes; samples too big for the tray;
  protons and neutrons too big to fit in the nucleus.
- Benches that look randomly dropped: put the workstation nearest the player without falling off the
  bench, reference data above it, operations in a row, fill empty space.
- Too dark to see; concentration labels too small; foreshortened readouts too small to read.
- Z-fighting and glitching edges (door hinge, tube base, tray edges, ship pad, reference strips).
- 2D pictures drawn under the 3D model so both show; weird black lines over cards.
- Realism: crates too new, trays that do not look like trays, a cutter that does not look like a cutter,
  textures that look like cracked earth painted blue instead of snow and ice.
- Superscripts that render oddly ("Mg 2+" should show a real superscript).
- Ordering that fights intuition: sort lowest to highest left to right; a heater dial that gets colder to
  the right.

## 6. Nothing visibly happens / the picture is wrong

- The hammer hits and only tiny text changes; the block picture must change.
- Running the tester should show an electron flying out or in, and revert when the sample is removed.
- Electrons leaving the metal should be seen as soon as the threshold is reached, faster with brighter
  light.
- Motion too fast to count electrons.
- The model contradicts the chemistry: steel drawn in neat rows (no interstitial atoms); two nitrogen
  electron pairs overlapping to look like one; red light "getting through" when the spectrum shows none;
  lone pairs that look like atoms; electrons printed on a 2D picture under moving ones.
- Use 3D where the idea is spatial (prediction cards as 3D models in mid-air, standing waves traced in 3D
  round the ring) and keep flat things flat (the Tallow lens display).

## 7. Explainers (reward animations)

- Animations, not interactives: no buttons, nothing to click.
- No text, labels or numbers inside the animation, except needed values such as bond angles.
- One concept at a time; "information overload" is a failure. Nothing there unless it is necessary.
- Show the actual mechanism close up: high-energy particles flying at an atom, most passing, a few
  bouncing off the nucleus; a proton bending between charge plates while a neutron flies straight;
  electrons filling shells one by one; a tetrahedral model morphing into bent as lone pairs swell.
- Remove a spinning 3D model that adds nothing; remove an explainer that repeats the last one.
- Fit the window without scrolling; slow down and exaggerate the key motion (highlight the moving
  electrons).

## 8. Tours

- Text and pointer disagree ("Drag the lens onto a dish" while the pointer puts a sample in the answer
  tray; a step about a lens on a stage with no lens; a step about a pan with no pan).
- Wrong order (drag the plate on first, then turn the dial).
- Steps that are identical, fragmented, or suggest a wrong move (putting the colour card in the cuvette;
  dragging the dropper into the spectrophotometer). The game should also refuse those wrong moves.
- The highlight must include the whole relevant thing (the scale with its reading; both atoms).
- Show the real gesture where it is new (drag the particle in, then press "Switch on"); just highlight
  where a gesture would mislead or give away the answer.
- No "how to submit" tours, and no tour for a gesture already taught; keep tours for the first time a
  student drags a dial, moves a card and so on.
- Explain unfamiliar lab equipment and procedures; never assume lab experience.

## 9. Debriefs and story

- Too much text at once: go one concept at a time, minimum text, with visuals (orbital shapes, real
  arrows rather than lines with balls).
- Remove story asides that confuse ("how chlorine got messed with") or mention a mystery the student has
  not met yet.
- Remove lines that overgeneralise or mislead (every atom forms a set number of bonds).
- The quest must actually resolve its mystery (it never said ionic bonds are much stronger than covalent
  ones, so the mystery made no sense).

## 10. Order and coverage

- Introduce the idea before the stage that relies on it: a discovery stage for the photoelectric effect
  before measuring it; Kelvin before gas-law proportions; wave-particle duality before the ring stage; a
  methane stage before ammonia so 109.5° has a source; full outer shell explained as s + p = 8 when
  stability is first mentioned.
- Predict stages sit immediately before the stage that tests them.
- Missing content: aufbau exceptions (Cr, Cu); why melting points differ and how to order them; the
  common orbital surface pictures; more stages sorting electrons by quantum numbers.
- Stages that make you guess ("I have no clue at what point each sample becomes molten") need a better
  way in, not a hint.
- Split a stage that does two things (predict a number, then confirm); merge or remove thin ones.

## 11. Screens and chrome

- Remove every line that is not needed to play: legends, "Click or press space to advance", "Avalon
  station, Flight deck directory", "Primary Objective", "Act ___", light-year distances, mastery
  explanations, "unranked" tags, feature lines.
- One skip button and one continue button; text itself should not be a button.
- Clicking a nav button should not change its look.
- Signs and doors must read correctly from the side you see them.
- Worked problems and drills get the whole screen (black backdrop, large text, calculator), not a small
  window over the 3D world.
